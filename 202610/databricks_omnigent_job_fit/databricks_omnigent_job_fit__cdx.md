### Important boundaries in the fit argument

**Workflow isolation and approvals are not proof of a security sandbox.** SASE uses separate repository workspaces, but its Codex provider contains approval-and-sandbox bypass arguments. Omnigent documents OS sandboxing and cloud execution environments. Present SASE's review and authorization architecture accurately; do not claim it demonstrates enterprise-grade containment, credential isolation, or least-privilege enforcement. [SASE Codex provider](https://github.com/sase-org/sase/blob/62604c10b7b01af1dd28cd43404d6728c2c3a375/src/sase/llm_provider/codex.py), [Omnigent execution prerequisites](https://github.com/omnigent-ai/omnigent/blob/ee2488aee1d458033f854871b40bf3107d2fb748/README.md).

**Deterministic orchestration testing is adjacent to agent evaluation, but does not establish it.** Failure fixtures and recorded tool outcomes are excellent infrastructure evidence. Agent Quality additionally needs useful, reproducible signals about probabilistic agent behavior. I did not establish that SASE has a statistically rigorous task-success benchmark, production feedback loop, or measured quality improvement from this inspection.

**A sophisticated project is not proof of enterprise production experience.** SASE's README explicitly calls it alpha. I did not establish your years of employment, distributed cloud operations, TypeScript/React depth, people or technical leadership, professional ML background, contributor-community growth, work authorization, salary preferences, or willingness to relocate. Treat those as facts to supply from your résumé, not qualifications inferred from SASE.

## How I would present your application

Lead with a concise project statement: **“I maintain an open-source control layer for multiple coding-agent harnesses, with durable execution state, reviewable outputs, explicit human decisions, and reproducible failure testing.”** Then connect two concrete design examples to the target role. For platform engineering, use adapter contracts and recovery after interrupted execution. For Agent Quality, use deterministic failures and the link between observed execution and trustworthy verification. For Omnigent advocacy, use a clear demo and evidence that somebody else successfully learned or contributed through your work.

Prepare a short portfolio walkthrough using the existing repository and demos. Explain one full lifecycle: task submission, workspace allocation, agent execution, failure or human review, verification, and final output. Name the failure modes and trade-offs rather than just enumerating features. Add measured outcomes only where you actually have them: time saved, externally active users or contributors, recovered failures, task success, and operational overhead.

A small Omnigent contribution or interoperability demo could strengthen your case, especially if it exercises harness capabilities or translates SASE's durable review model into an Omnigent workflow. This is an application recommendation, not work performed in this research. The shipped harness conformance bench is a concrete area where your experience might be useful; verify an actual issue or maintainer need before investing heavily.

For a recruiter conversation, ask whether **AI Platform, Agent Quality, or the Omnigent team** has a home for an engineer focused on cross-harness orchestration and agent reliability, and which level and location fit your professional record. If your preference is engineering, say that directly rather than accepting a DevRel role merely to work near Omnigent. I made no applications and contacted nobody.
