# Independent audit of recent bob-cli task-bead impact

Researcher: **cdx**. Audit cutoff: **2026-10-07 16:24:36 UTC**. Inclusion window: **2026-10-05 16:24:36 UTC through 2026-10-07 16:24:36 UTC**, inclusive.

The highest-impact work in this cohort protects the daily task workflow and the means of delivering changes: removing unnecessary query timeouts, preventing plugin rollbacks, repairing artifact-link writes, preserving review-walk identity, and restoring trustworthy Rust verification. The strongest growth opportunities make Bryan's existing reading knowledge complete and searchable. The final ranking includes completed work because the question asks which beads are associated with impactful work, rather than only which outstanding tasks to execute next.

## Scope, evidence, and method

I enumerated **all 109 task beads in the current project's effective store**, across every status, with:

```sh
sase bead list --status all --type task --limit 0 --format json
```

A bead qualifies when either its `created_at` or a `plus_one_evidence[].timestamp` falls inside the fixed window. A recent `updated_at`, an ordinary note, or an older +1 does not qualify by itself. The +1 recording timestamp is the appropriate inclusion timestamp; `observed_since` describes the reporter's observation provenance rather than when the bead was +1ed. I bounded both sides of the window so later activity cannot silently change this audit.

The resulting cohort is **31 beads: 25 recently created, 9 recently +1ed, with 3 in both groups**. Six older beads enter solely through recent +1 evidence. There are **23 +1 evidence entries in the window**. Statuses are **28 ready and 3 closed**; types are **9 bugs, 4 flakes, 3 CI failures, 8 memory tasks, and 7 features**. Plans and phase beads are outside the requested task-bead population. No current-workspace store was merged with sibling workspaces or legacy stores.

I performed an audited `sase bead read` of every eligible bead, including its typed fields, creation reason, notes, closure evidence, and corroboration. Empty descriptions on several newer beads do not mean missing evidence: their substantive reports live in `task_type_fields`. I also read the canonical bead/artifact conventions and relevant glossary definitions through `sase memory read`.

Source checks used bob-cli commit **65501f6977144ff8c7b431e41b47e2d462668bad**, plus the linked bob-plugins checkout opened through `sase repo open`, at **2fb377055daef8623b04ada90e3ed155d4eb2cc6**. Source inspection confirmed the eager JavaScript initialization, environment-test isolation gap, plugin-source selection, missing verification recipe, completion coverage gap, library coverage boundary, ref-job recovery design, and shipped marker/URL fixes. The bead ledger is the fixed initial data snapshot; source checks are observations of those checkout revisions, not a claim that every prior live-vault observation was remeasured.

This was independent research. I did not locate or read the other reports in this swarm, contact peers, or obtain their findings through transcripts or summaries. Historical evidence already recorded on project beads is shared input material. I did not modify task statuses, append +1s, repair stores, deploy plugins, migrate the vault, or create follow-up beads.

### How impact was judged

The ranking is an ordinal judgment, not a claimed financial estimate. I favored:

- **Daily workflow reach:** whether the work changes the reliability of planning, review, capture, or knowledge retrieval.
- **Severity and invisibility:** silently skipping real work, reverting deployed behavior, or excluding known material weighs more than a cosmetic mismatch.
- **Delivery leverage:** defects that repeatedly obstruct many independent landings have project-wide cost.
- **Durable value:** unlocking hundreds of reading records or searching annotations compounds beyond one interaction.
- **Evidence confidence:** current source mechanisms, independent reproductions, and quantified scope carry more weight than speculative benefits.

Size is a planning signal, not impact. A small repair can protect a large workflow; an xlarge migration can offer substantial value while requiring careful review. +1s increase confidence in occurrence and breadth; they are append-only evidence, not popularity votes, measured incident rates, or independent units of economic value. Likewise, “113 of 115 notes” and “about 424 records” are recorded measurements, not new measurements made by this audit.

## Cross-bead findings and limits

**The Rust environment-test problem should be treated as one remediation program.** `bob-cli-2e` and `bob-cli-40` name the same `capture_pomodoros` missing-note test and its `BOB_DAY_FILE` interference. The former already contains the more specific cross-module diagnosis. The five recent +1s on `40` and one on `2e` corroborate the work; they do not justify occupying two ranking slots. The new `bob-cli-5c` adds a distinct `note_ready` failure. Its `BOB_NOW` sensitivity is consistent with the same class of shared environment problem, and an older `2e` corroboration explicitly describes that mechanism, but this audit did not experimentally isolate its cause. Keep that test in the acceptance scope without assuming the narrow day-file fix will necessarily fix it.

**A green gate requires several independent repairs.** `bob-cli-4j` is the universal completion-kind defect; `bob-cli-4u` is a deterministic Pandoc-dependent assertion mismatch; `bob-cli-2e`/`40` are parallel interference; `bob-cli-3c` is an absent wrapper recipe. Fixing one does not fix the others. Current `justfile:6` already has `all: fmt lint test`, so the missing `check` entry point creates substantial workflow friction but does not mean the project has no verification commands. I reproduced its absence with `just --show check`, which exited 1 before any gate ran. `5b` is a separate browser self-test problem; its recorded evidence says production clips already use a short scratch-directory fallback.

**The three eligible closes are real completed outcomes, not backlog to reopen.** `4m` restores review correctness; `4r` cleans generated annotations; `4v` restores private-address rejection. Current source contains the fixes for `4r` and `4v`. I ran the linked plugin's six `walk identity` regression tests for `4m`; **6/6 passed**, including line-shifting successor selection and stale-key handling. I did not rerun the entire Rust suite, browser self-test, Mac CI, or live-vault measurements. Their historical execution claims are attributed to bead evidence.

**Library work has two different multipliers.** `4x` expands what the index knows; `4y` expands what can be learned from indexed annotations. They reinforce one another, but annotation search is useful on the existing library before migration. The feature evidence reports 594 indexed notes and roughly 424 unindexed legacy records; these are different units, so I do not add them into a fabricated “total library size” or coverage percentage. `50` solves a narrower history problem: a reopened reference loses the evidence of having been finished in the current derived-state view. That is worthwhile, but it requires a new history design rather than treating today's queued state as proof of never having read something.

**Ref-job scheduling is the more immediate resilience improvement than retries.** Source already has a worker lock, stale-running recovery, stuck-fallback recovery, and a post-unlock pending check. `5g` adds another occasion to run those mechanisms after a missed kick or stopped worker. `5f` adds a new retry policy and eventual-fallback semantics. A scheduled drain cannot guarantee a 15-minute bound while the Mac is asleep or cron is unavailable, and it does not itself add network retries. Neither bead includes measured production failure frequency, so their benefits remain an inference from the current execution design. The Mac crontab problem referenced as `bob-cli-30` is related context, not an eligible candidate in this audit.

**Security and memory work were considered rather than omitted.** `4v` fixes a genuine validation bypass, and the same landing notes also report resolved-address checks and curl pinning. The bead explicitly describes low exposure because Bryan chooses URLs; it is completed and narrowly scoped, so I do not infer a remotely exploitable service or elevate it above broader observed failures. The eight memory beads preserve accepted contracts or repair glossary drift. `5d`, `51`, `5a`, and `4t` have the strongest architectural value in that group, but the shipped behavior is already documented elsewhere and no current product malfunction is established by their absence. This research does not authorize changing those memories or reversing accepted designs.

The closest outstanding alternatives to the selected ten are `4u` and `3c`, followed by `3w`, `5b`, and `5f`. For an execution-only queue, the two completed entries in the final ranking would leave room for `4u` and `3c`. That does not change the requested ranking of the corresponding work's overall impact. Ordering within the leading cluster is less certain than the distinction between broad workflow failures and narrow polish or documentation tasks.

## Ranked list: the ten most impactful eligible task beads

1. **bob-cli-33 — Remove the unnecessary JavaScript deadline from ordinary task queries.** **Ready · bug · medium.** This protects the central read path used by planning, freshness, query commands, budget checks, and the tmux plan display. Queries without any JavaScript clause still hydrate the entire task index inside the same two-second budget intended for a user expression, so busy hosts can lose otherwise valid views of real work. The recent October 7 reproduction failed on the live vault even though fixture tests passed, making this a particularly consequential reliability gap. Current `src/native/dataview/tasks/mod.rs:167,195,240,277` eagerly constructs the sandbox, and `js.rs:30,185,241` confirms the timeout and whole-index hydration. Avoiding unnecessary initialization would improve both reliability and read latency. **Confidence: high in the mechanism and reported failure; no failure-rate or latency savings estimate is claimed.** Evidence: `bead:bob-cli-33`, including its October 7 14:26:57 +1.

2. **bob-cli-59 — Prevent plugin sync from deploying the wrong checkout and rolling back other work.** **Ready · bug · small.** An agent can successfully implement or deploy a plugin change, then a routine bare sync deploys the canonical checkout and restores older sibling plugin behavior. The recorded incident reverted ledger-tools **1.34.0 to 1.33.0** during the Task Link lane-toggle landing. This directly threatens the reliability of Bryan's active task interface and breaks the assumption that completed agent work remains deployed. Source confirms that `src/native/env.rs:18` chooses the configured/canonical repository, `plugins/cli.rs:224` falls back to it, and sync pulls before copying. A pre-copy refusal in the mismatched-checkout case would protect the whole plugin suite with a comparatively narrow change. **Confidence: high in source selection and the recorded rollback; deployment frequency is unmeasured.** Evidence: `bead:bob-cli-59` and its creation report; there are no +1s yet.

3. **bob-cli-21 — Repair the artifact-link store corruption that blocks new relationships and plan handoffs.** **Ready · bug · large.** This is a persistent horizontal failure in the engineering workflow. New links fail regardless of the new source/target pair, blocking evidence association, related-task links, and plan proposals with link inlets. The historical October 4 evidence describes a proposal consuming its scratch file into the archive before failing, leaving no approval gate or handoff; the two eligible October 6 +1s show that research and epic landings still hit the same fixed operation ID. Repair and defensive publication ordering would restore traceability and prevent partial proposal completion across future work. Its implementation locus is SASE/store handling, though it is tracked as a bob-cli project bead. **Confidence: high from five lifetime independent corroborations, including two in-window; I did not perform a mutating link-write reproduction.** Evidence: `bead:bob-cli-21`, especially October 6 19:12:27 and 19:56:55 +1s.

4. **bob-cli-4m — Preserve review-row identity after line-shifting edits.** **Closed, done · bug · medium.** This work prevents the daily review walk from silently treating an unrelated due row as already answered after insertion, recurring-task completion, or another line-shifting edit. Silent omission matters more than an obvious error because Bryan can finish a review while believing all due work was seen. The shipped fix checks note/text identity instead of trusting day-long `path:line` accumulation and resolves the true successor after edits. The closing note names navigation 2.7.1 and dedicated regressions; this audit independently ran the six current `walk identity` tests, all passing. **Confidence: high; this is delivered value and should stay closed.** Evidence: `bead:bob-cli-4m`, its October 6 14:42:51 close, and bob-plugins `536-plugin-review-advance.js` plus `test-navigation-review-advance.cjs`.

5. **bob-cli-2e — Make environment-dependent Rust tests share valid process-wide isolation.** **Ready · bug · small.** This is the strongest representative of the repeated test-race cluster. A module-private mutex in `capture_complete` cannot protect another module's unlocked `BOB_DAY_FILE` mutations or ambient reads. Full parallel runs repeatedly fail while isolated tests pass; historical evidence includes **5/5 full-run failures on one landing** and an observation that cargo stops before CLI tests run. Fourteen lifetime +1s, including one on October 7, and the five recent corroborations on the overlapping `bob-cli-40` show repeated delivery cost. Repairing shared state isolation would reduce reruns, preserve useful signal, and allow integration checks to run reliably across many independent epics. Include the distinct `5c` test in investigation and verification rather than assume its cause. **Confidence: high for the day-file race; medium for extending the same fix to `note_ready`.** Evidence: `bead:bob-cli-2e`, corroborating `bead:bob-cli-40`, related `bead:bob-cli-5c`; current `capture_pomodoros.rs:1189,1277` and `capture_complete/tests/mod.rs:24`.

6. **bob-cli-4j — Restore deterministic completion coverage for `ref create --audio`.** **Ready · CI failure · small.** A single missing completion decision makes the Rust library gate fail consistently across otherwise unrelated changes, so it has much more impact than its apparent shell-completion surface suggests. Six in-window +1s independently reproduce the same failure across completion, inbox routing, reference library, freshness, and URL-routing landings. The title uses the older `highlights create` name; current failures identify **`ref create:audio`**. Source still defines `Arg::new("audio")` without a nontrivial value hint and the coverage test requires a decision. A correct file-path completion hint/table entry would remove a universal false baseline and improve the actual completion behavior. **Confidence: high and repeatedly deterministic; fixing this alone will not clear the separate Pandoc or race failures.** Evidence: `bead:bob-cli-4j`; `src/native/highlights_ref/create.rs:221` and `src/native/completion/kinds.rs:465`.

7. **bob-cli-4x — Bring roughly 424 legacy reading records into the reference-library index.** **Ready · feature · xlarge.** This is the largest knowledge-completeness opportunity in the cohort. Hundreds of records, including 75 in `work_ref.md` and 69 in `nvim_ref.md` in the recorded inventory, are invisible to `bob ref find/list`. Bringing them into scope improves reading-history lookup, duplicate avoidance, and agent recommendations grounded in material Bryan already encountered. The current library explicitly warns that absence is not proof of never having read something; migration reduces that blind spot without pretending today's index is complete. Current `ref_library/coverage.rs` supplies an inventory and excludes already mirrored `(source_block, source_path)` pairs. The bulk vault write needs a reviewed mapping, deduplication, and source-line policy; the value is substantial but not a trivial cleanup. **Confidence: high in the coverage gap; record counts are attributed measurements and exact migration outcomes depend on design.** Evidence: `bead:bob-cli-4x`, `docs/ref.md` Coverage, and `src/native/ref_library/coverage.rs`.

8. **bob-cli-4r — Keep exported metadata out of Bryan's annotations.** **Closed, done · bug · large.** The recorded audit found marker mirrors leaking into **113 of 115 annotated reference notes**, often presenting stale status/parent/URL metadata as user-authored notes. That widespread contamination damages both human interpretation and agent retrieval: a metadata echo should not count as something Bryan highlighted or wrote. The delivered content-based detection and preamble handling restore semantic integrity at the sync boundary and remove leaked blocks without tombstones. Current `region.rs` and `sidecar_render.rs` contain the repair; the close reports dedicated unit/CLI tests and **1047/1047 CLI tests passing**. **Confidence: high in the delivered source mechanism; the 113/115 live-vault scope and full-suite result come from recorded evidence, not this audit's rerun. Keep closed.** Evidence: `bead:bob-cli-4r`, its October 7 01:38:24 close, and `src/native/highlights_ref/{region,sidecar_render}.rs`.

9. **bob-cli-4y — Search annotations across the whole reference library.** **Ready · feature · large.** This converts the library from a tool that reads a reference already identified into a way to discover where Bryan discussed an idea. Search over highlights, comments, and standalone notes would make accumulated reading useful during research and implementation, with page/block provenance allowing an agent to return to the original evidence. The reported existing index covers 594 notes; the structured annotation parser and read-only rows already provide substantial foundations. It compounds with `4x`, but does not need migration to be useful. Ranking and output-size semantics still need design, and the audit cannot quantify how often cross-library search would be used. **Confidence: medium-high in the capability gap and enabling code, medium in realized value because it is a proposed feature.** Evidence: `bead:bob-cli-4y`, `docs/ref.md` single-reference `show` contract, and `src/native/highlights_ref/region.rs`.

10. **bob-cli-5g — Give durable reference jobs a scheduled recovery opportunity on the Mac.** **Ready · feature · small.** The new bare-link reading workflow persists intent, but processing is normally triggered by a detached kick. A failed spawn, reboot, or interrupted worker can leave that intent waiting until another kick or manual repair. Running the existing single-flight drain before the existing 15-minute `ref scan` gives pending, stale-running, and stuck-fallback jobs a recurring recovery path and lets successfully clipped material enter the library in the same scheduled cycle. The worker already implements the recovery and locking; the missing piece is regular invocation and documentation in the chezmoi-managed schedule. That makes this a small change with potentially broad capture reliability value. It does not add retry/backoff, and the cadence depends on the Mac actually running its schedule. **Confidence: high in the current recovery design, medium in operational payoff because missed-kick frequency was not measured.** Evidence: `bead:bob-cli-5g`, `docs/ref-jobs.md`, and `src/native/ref_jobs/{kick,worker}.rs`.
